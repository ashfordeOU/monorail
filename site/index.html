FAQ

Good questions

What is a scrolling window manager?

A window manager that puts your windows side by side on a strip that runs past the edges of your screen. A new window slides in next to the one you're using, and every other window keeps its size: you scroll along the strip, with the keyboard, the trackpad or the slider, instead of squeezing everything into one screen or digging through overlapping windows.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every feature, including docking, tab groups, saved views, scripting and every display, with no trial, subscription or window limits. There's no licence key to buy, move between Macs or renew for the next major version: install it on every Mac you own, and every update is free.

Is it safe to install?

Yes. Every release is signed with Ashforde's Apple Developer ID and notarised by Apple, so macOS opens it without warnings. Safe to install shows how to check a download yourself.

Does Monorail turn off SIP or change the Dock?

No. System Integrity Protection stays on, there's no kernel extension, and nothing is injected into the Dock or other apps. Monorail arranges windows the way Apple intends, through the Accessibility permission, which you can take back at any time in System Settings.

Which Macs does it run on?

Every Mac with macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and on Intel: one universal app, with the same features on both.

Can I use Monorail on a work Mac without admin rights?

Yes. It installs without an administrator into your own Applications folder. Stay Awake, Keep me Available, Evening Mode, the display controls and arranging scriptable apps such as Safari, Finder and Terminal work straight away. Arranging every window needs Accessibility, which only an administrator can allow on a managed Mac, and IT can approve it for Monorail in one step: the guide for IT.

Will it fight my other window tools?

Quit other window managers first; Settings → General → Mac setup checks for them. Stage Manager is fine to keep: Monorail pauses while it's on and carries on when you turn it off.

What happens to my windows if I quit?

Every window comes back on screen. Next time Monorail starts, your layout returns.

Does it work with multiple displays, projectors and Sidecar?

Yes: each display gets its own strip, an unplugged display's windows come back when you reconnect, and Sidecar iPads fit every slide.

Which languages?

English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified), Portuguese, Korean and Italian.

I use AeroSpace, yabai or Rectangle. Can I bring my setup?

Yes. Settings → Basics → Import Settings… brings over your shortcuts, gaps, floating apps and workspace rules.

Is there a Windows or Linux version?

Yes, in beta: Monorail for Windows (11 and 10, no installer or administrator rights) and Monorail for GNOME (45 or later). They're numbered like the Mac version and come out with it.

Can I script it?

Yes: monorail:// links, the monorail command-line tool, a local API, an MCP server for AI assistants, and AppleScript or JavaScript, with a gallery of scripts to start from.

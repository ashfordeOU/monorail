Privacy

Your Mac’s business stays on your Mac.

Monorail has no account, and nothing about your windows, files or what you do leaves your Mac. Last updated 13 November 2026.

Never leaves your Mac

  • Window titles, app names and what’s on your screen
  • Your clipboard, including images and the text in them
  • Meeting Notes: what was said, the transcript and the summary
  • Files, file names and paths
  • What you type or say to Monorail
  • Battery, app and system history

Sent, and only this

  • Anonymous usage counts, once a day, which you can turn off
  • A daily update check, which you can turn off
  • A small list of known app quirks
  • What you turn on yourself: AI Usage, Weather, the assistant, webhooks, sync, the phone remote

Permissions

Monorail needs one permission, Accessibility, which macOS requires before any app can move other apps’ windows. You can revoke it at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

macOS asks for others only when you turn on the feature that needs one: Screen Recording (live previews, sharing one slide, and hearing the other people in Meeting Notes), Calendars (your day and opening video calls), Microphone and Speech Recognition (voice commands and Meeting Notes, recognised on your Mac), Camera (the camera tile), Local Network (the phone remote and layout sync). Window pictures and text are read on your Mac and never stored or sent anywhere.

Anonymous usage counts

On from the start and sent once a day, so we know how many Macs run Monorail and what to improve: a random install ID (not tied to you, your Apple ID or your Mac’s serial number), the Monorail and macOS versions, the Mac model, chip type and core count, a memory range, the number of displays, the system language, how Monorail was installed and which features are on. Never window titles, app names, file names, what you type or say, or anything from your screen. The server doesn’t store IP addresses.

Turn it off in Settings → Devices & Sharing → Trust → Share anonymous usage counts. The app shows exactly what’s sent under What’s Sent….

Update checks and app quirks

Monorail checks monorail.ashforde.org for updates once a day; turn it off in Settings → General. It also fetches a small file of known app quirks that helps it handle apps that behave unusually.

Features that talk to other services, only when you turn them on

  • AI Usage asks the services you add (such as Claude, Codex, OpenRouter or DeepSeek) for your limits and balances, using your own sign-in or key, straight from your Mac. Keys stay in your Keychain. Logs from tools like Codex, Gemini, OpenClaw and Hermes, and models in Ollama and LM Studio, are read on your Mac.
  • Weather asks Open-Meteo for the forecast at the places you add, by their coordinates. It never asks for your location.
  • The assistant works only with your own API key for the provider you choose (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI or OpenRouter), and sends just the sentence it didn’t understand, the names of open apps and Monorail’s list of commands, straight to that provider. With Ollama on your Mac, nothing leaves the Mac.
  • Webhooks, the phone remote, layout sync and iCloud sync send what they say, where you point them.

On your Mac only

Meeting Notes turns speech into text on your Mac with Apple’s speech recognition, keeps no audio, and writes summaries with Apple’s on-device model on macOS 26. Notes are kept on your Mac as Markdown. The clipboard keeps its history on your Mac, never keeps copies from password managers, and hides anything that looks like a key, token or card number until you click to show it. Battery measurements are worked out on your Mac from figures macOS already keeps, and never sent anywhere.

Crash reports

Crash and hang reports stay on your Mac unless you choose to send them. After an unexpected quit, Monorail may ask what happened and start an email to contact@ashforde.org for you to review: it contains the Monorail and macOS versions and where it crashed, never window titles, files, paths or names, and nothing is sent until you submit it.

This website

The site sets no cookies. It counts each page view and each download (which file and version), with the site you came from, such as Product Hunt, and your country. It keeps no IP addresses and nothing that identifies you or links one visit to another. Browsers that send Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control aren’t counted at all. The install script counts its downloads the same way; set MONORAIL_NO_COUNT=1 to skip it.

If you choose an appearance or colour with the palette button, that choice (and whether you closed the Product Hunt welcome) is kept in your browser’s local storage on this device only, so the site looks the same next time. It is never sent anywhere; clear it by choosing Automatic and the Monorail colour, or by clearing the site’s data.
